## Coverage and limits

The three club histories are grounded in the clubs’ official history pages, championship archive, and tournament retrospectives. Cardozo’s four lessons now include a playing-career timeline, two individual milestones (his scoring records and 2004 Olympic tournament), and the 1998 final. Chivas’s opened 2018 biography provides the playing-club timeline and four scoring-title years; FIFA and Concacaf support the individual record and all-time scorer facts. The Toluca pages support the 1998 and 1999 final context. No full career appearance or goal total is asserted. Club America and Chivas player paths use official club match reports, milestone pages, and archived match accounts. Toluca’s official 1998, 1999, and 2008 title pages support Cardozo- and Cristante-specific details. The official Cristante biography supports his birthplace, early goalkeeping ambition, and 2002 coaching studies, but does not give a complete club-by-club playing timeline or retirement date. Accordingly the career lesson covers only those biographical facts; no club-arrival/departure sequence, career appearances, clean sheets, or league-minute record is asserted. The Hall of Fame page supports five listed league honours (two Clausura plus three Apertura), while Toluca’s official retrospectives support the penalty-save facts. The duplicate Alejandro Vela question was removed from Cristante’s 2008 rebound lesson; it now asks which title number Toluca secured after the save. Toluca’s Cardozo pages do not give trustworthy full-career goal totals, so none are asserted.

For Cardozo’s Verano 1998 final, Toluca says Necaxa won the first leg 2–1 and Toluca then won the home leg 5–2. The 5–2 is explicitly the home-leg match score, not the aggregate. The official archive does not support Cardozo goal-minute details, so none are asserted. For the 1999 final, the opened official retrospective supports the 5–5 aggregate, extra time, penalties and Cristante’s decisive stop. Cardozo’s 1999 final facts were removed from his personal path because the source did not support enough personal actions for a distinct player lesson; his career chapter now uses the opened Chivas player timeline.

Chivas used the name Estadio Chivas for the 2017 final; Estadio Akron is a later name. The 2010 Chivas–Manchester United opening match took place at Estadio Omnilife; Javier Hernández scored the stadium’s first goal. The lesson preserves these contemporaneous names instead of applying the current venue name retroactively.

For Ochoa’s first América title and the 30-match unbeaten run, the opened FMF biography page was federation-hosted but did not render in the text extractor; the claims were visible in the federation page’s indexed result and are limited to those claims. His debut date was checked against Club América’s official video description. FIFA’s official article supports the six World Cup editions beginning with Germany 2006; prompts say he reached/was included in editions and do not imply that he played in every tournament.

The Club América 1981–1990 history page also did not open in the reader.
